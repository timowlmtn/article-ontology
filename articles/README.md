# Articles

Each article has a bounded research context. Copy `000-template/` to the next numbered directory, give it a descriptive slug, and begin with the brief and questions. An article may draw on project-wide sources and ontology notes, but its `sources.md` and `evidence.md` should expose exactly what the argument relies on.

Do not treat a directory number as a publication commitment. The writer decides the thesis, sequence, and readiness.

## Article file contract

| File | Purpose | Ready to advance when… |
|---|---|---|
| `brief.md` | Scope, audience, stakes, thesis, and change-of-mind conditions | The inquiry is bounded and the thesis is explicitly tentative |
| `questions.md` | Research agenda and evidence requirements | Each important uncertainty has a next action |
| `sources.md` | Accepted article corpus | Every relied-upon source has a stable project source ID |
| `evidence.md` | Claim-level support, challenge, and interpretation | Major claims have locators, counterevidence, and honest statuses |
| `field-notes.md` | Raw observation, experience, and writer questions | Observation is separated from later interpretation |
| `synthesis.md` | Cross-source findings and thesis revision | Agreements, disputes, gaps, and inferences are visible |
| `outline.md` | Argument flow and evidence burden | Every section states what it must establish |
| `draft.md` | Writer-controlled prose | Claims can be traced backward to evidence or clearly labeled reflection |
| `fact-check.md` | Final verification and fairness audit | All material checks are complete or explicitly unresolved |

## Status flow

`framing` → `researching` → `reading` → `mapping evidence` → `synthesizing` → `outlining` → `drafting` → `verifying` → `writer review`

Movement is not strictly linear. New drafting questions should flow backward into `questions.md` or `evidence.md`, not be answered from memory or plausible AI prose.

## Context boundary

When asking AI for help, name the article and the stage. Unless broader comparison is required, provide context in this order: brief; questions and evidence; accepted source notes; relevant ontology entries; draft. This keeps research bounded and reduces accidental contamination from unrelated articles.

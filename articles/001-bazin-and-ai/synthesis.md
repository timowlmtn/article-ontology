## Provisional tensions to investigate

- **Origin versus appearance:** A generated image may look photographic without documenting a corresponding event.
- **Trace versus sense:** Bazin's continuing relevance may exceed a narrow account of physical indexicality.
- **Auteur versus collaboration:** Singular authorship can reveal recurring judgment while obscuring distributed labor and constraints.
- **Automation versus agency:** A mechanical or computational process does not eliminate human choice; it redistributes where choices occur.
- **Ontology versus ontology:** A theory of an image's being and a formal domain model are not the same artifact or inquiry.
- **Criticism versus theory application:** Concrete works may revise the conceptual framework rather than merely exemplify it.

## What cannot yet be claimed

- That Bazin would reject or embrace AI-generated images.
- That all generative images lack meaningful relations to reality.
- That auteur theory assigns absolute authorship to directors in every formulation.
- That provenance is sufficient to restore documentary trust.
- That computational ontology solves the aesthetic or philosophical problems raised by Bazin.

## Strongest challenge to the provisional thesis

- **Challenge:** A causal/indexical reading may flatten Bazin. His realism may be better understood through sense, ambiguity, contingency, spectatorship, or ethical attention, allowing his thought to illuminate synthetic media without requiring analog photographic origin.

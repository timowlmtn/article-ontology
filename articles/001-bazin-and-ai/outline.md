# Provisional article flow — Bazin and AI

This is a research map, not an approved outline. Rebuild it after `synthesis.md` contains accepted, locator-level evidence and the writer's restored thesis.

## Reader movement

- **Opening understanding:** AI imagery seems to pose a new problem of authorship or falsity.
- **Turning point:** Bazin reveals that the deeper problem concerns an image's origin, its relation to a world, and the spectator's grounds for belief—but Bazin himself cannot be reduced to analog trace.
- **Closing understanding:** The useful division may concern disclosed relations among reality, mediation, judgment, and accountability rather than a simple human/machine boundary.

## 1. Encounter: an object before a thesis

- **Establish:** A concrete museum object makes “what kind of thing is this?” experiential before it becomes technical vocabulary.
- **Evidence / observation:** RISD field notes and verified museum record.
- **Risk:** Decorative scene-setting that bears no argumentative weight.
- **Transition:** Move from “what am I looking at?” to “what must have happened for this to exist?”

## 2. One word, three inquiries

- **Establish:** Philosophical, Bazinian, and computational ontology answer different questions.
- **Claims:** CLM-001.
- **Sources:** SRC-001, SRC-009, SRC-010.
- **Objection:** The distinction may be too tidy; acknowledge internal disputes.
- **Transition:** Narrow from ontology in general to Bazin's image.

## 3. Bazin's wager about the photographic image

- **Establish:** Photographic production changes the relation among image, object, artist, and belief.
- **Claims:** CLM-002.
- **Sources:** SRC-001, SRC-002.
- **Necessary distinctions:** origin / resemblance; automatic process / absence of human choice; evidence / truth.
- **Transition:** Ask how an ontological claim becomes an aesthetic preference.

## 4. Ontology becomes criticism

- **Establish:** Bazin's realism operates through film form, ambiguity, and spectatorship, not surface resemblance alone.
- **Claims:** CLM-003, CLM-004.
- **Sources:** SRC-002, SRC-005, SRC-007, selected reviews and scene.
- **Objection:** Do not turn long take or deep focus into mechanical guarantees of truth.
- **Transition:** If form organizes a viewer's freedom, whose organizing intelligence is it?

## 5. The auteur problem

- **Establish:** Auteurism developed as a contested critical practice, not a settled proof that one person creates a film.
- **Claims:** CLM-005.
- **Sources:** SRC-003, Bazin/Truffaut primary texts, SRC-004, Kael.
- **Necessary distinctions:** policy / theory; authorship / value; signature / control; responsibility / ownership.
- **Transition:** AI intensifies a problem cinema already contained: how to attribute organizing judgment within distributed production.

## 6. The synthetic break

- **Establish:** A chosen generative system can produce photographic appearance without requiring the pictured event.
- **Claims:** CLM-002, CLM-006.
- **Sources:** Primary technical documentation and a process-rich case study.
- **Objection:** Generated images still have causal histories and relations to real data, people, and institutions.
- **Transition:** The question shifts from whether there is a relation to reality to what kind of relation it is and whether it is visible.

## 7. The strongest Bazinian counterargument

- **Establish:** Bazin may remain relevant through sense, ambiguity, contingency, and attention even where analog indexicality fails.
- **Claims:** CLM-007.
- **Sources:** SRC-005, SRC-007, SRC-008.
- **Purpose:** Revise the thesis rather than stage a disposable objection.
- **Transition:** Ask what grounds belief and responsibility if appearance no longer carries origin on its face.

## 8. Provenance, judgment, and accountable authorship

- **Establish:** A better criticism of AI work maps origin, mediation, selection, form, disclosure, and accountability.
- **Claims:** CLM-006, CLM-008.
- **Evidence burden:** Specific case, provenance research, and a clear normative argument.
- **Objection:** Provenance can become a technical substitute for criticism or an unreliable badge of trust.
- **Transition:** Return to the museum object with a changed question.

## 9. Return: what kind of encounter remains?

- **Establish:** State the revised thesis at the limit of the evidence.
- **Observation:** Only if the RISD experience genuinely changed the argument.
- **Closing question:** What should criticism attend to when an image's visible form no longer tells us enough about how it came to be?

# Who is Bazin and why his ideas of Ontology are relevant when thinking about AI

As I thought about how knowledge and meaning relate to the real world, I 
found myself drawn to Ontologies, and ultimately, I discovered Bazin.

Bazin was a mid-twentieth century film critic who published a series of
magazines that ultimately became one of the foundational texts for modern
film criticism.

So how does this relate to AI.  As it turns out, Bazin was tackling with 
a similiar that we see in our transitional where AI is a part of everything,
except that in his world, art was transitioning to the photographic and
moving image.

As a keen thinker, he sought to give philosophical rigor to this transition
and came up with the notion of Ontology, or the study of what something truly is.

In Bazin's case, he pondering who is the artist in the making of the film.
In an extreme case, for example a documentary, do we really think of the 
artist as the one holds the camera and records?  Then doesn't that make 
anyone who can hold a camera and record an event an artist?

The idea has a deeper question underlying and delves into the event
being captured and what is real about it, or what is the essense of it.

Bazin writes:

    Originality in photography as distinct from originality in painting
    lies in the essentially objective character of photography.

There are striking similarities here to the use of AI in writing, where
the originality of the author from non AI writing is from the originality
of the prompt.  As the photography picks the angle, lighting, and 
exact timing of the photo, the AI writer works with prompts and AI to 
construct an essage.

The crucial difference, is that the photographer's subject is grounded in
reality, and the AI is grounded in the trained statistical environment
of the LLM - model that has synthesized thousands of books, web pages,
scholarly articles, reddit, etc.

The AI generation of reality doesn't even need reality behind it, and
yet, I could ask an AI to generate a picture of something I know is 
real, or upload a picture, and it would enhance it.

I could ask the AI to write an article and it would not have
any part of me or my ideas in that article, except the the initial prompt,
and yet, but guiding it to what I want, does my desire of an end-result
count as an original thought or the basis?

Because AI is not grounded in reality, the notion that AI can create
facsimiles of reality, like a photograph or a work that a real human
produced by thinking, drawing, typing - some physical act - it further
complicates the question beyond the "authenticity" of work with the
help of AI.

So then what of creativity?  When we do create something, there is a 
physical that we do that brings it into being.  When I type the essay
with my fingers, the words I am thinking follow on the document.  And
yet, always in the background, the AI is watching those words and can 
offer to change them for clarity, can point out where I'm mistaken, where
my ideas need development, and it is a non-human thing guiding
my toward a creative product.

The question is in the gap between truth and a representation of truth.
When we read something, or watch a movie, we guage the truth of it based
on whether we believe it.  It might be fiction, but as in the case of
The Bicycle Thief that Bazin writes about, "Nothing happens in Ladri 
de Biciclette"  that might just well as not have happened."

There is an ephermeral nature of the plot that makes it a beautiful and
and artistic film, in that it represents a dramatic and heartbreaking 
reality. And if we imagine the calculations of an AI direct recreating 
that film and each angle based on capturing the most dramatic angle,
the most dramatic event, and the following dramatic outcome to a conclusion,
could it be possible, other than in a trivial way that I can image in.

# RISD Museum field notes — Bazin and AI

The visit is a method for steeping the writer in material and historical encounters with art. It does not have to become a published scene.

## Suggested movement

Move by problems rather than by a story of inevitable progress:

1. preservation, ritual, and the persistence of bodies or objects;
2. representation, likeness, and symbolic form;
3. workshop production, copying, attribution, and anonymous labor;
4. perspective, optics, mechanical aids, and changing visual authority;
5. photography, film, sequence, duration, and evidence;
6. abstraction, construction, and images that refuse resemblance;
7. procedural, digital, or computational work where available.

Older works already complicate originality, solitary genius, automation, and authorship. Do not make AI the inevitable endpoint of the walk.

## Object encounter template

### Object — provisional description

- **Gallery / location:**
- **Accession or label information:** Photograph or transcribe; verify later in the museum catalog.
- **Raw perception:** What did I see before reading the label?
- **Material presence:** What is physically here?
- **Mediation:** Which materials, tools, conventions, people, and institutions made it visible?
- **Authorship:** Whose decisions are recognized? Whose labor disappears?
- **Representational act:** Does it resemble, record, construct, symbolize, preserve, perform, or generate?
- **Temporal relation:** What event, process, body, or previous object does it claim or imply?
- **Spectator freedom:** What does the work determine, and what does it leave open?
- **Bazin question:** Would Bazin care most about what this depicts, how it was caused, how it organizes attention, or the ambiguity it preserves?
- **AI question:** What changes if a system can produce the visible form without the same material history?
- **Resistance:** How does this object trouble the thesis rather than illustrate it?
- **Writer language:** Preserve an unpolished sentence or image.

## Every three objects

- What pattern am I starting to impose?
- Which object refuses that pattern?
- Am I confusing resemblance with origin?
- Am I treating “the artist” as a single causal explanation?
- Is the museum label changing the ontology I attribute to the work?

## Voice-note protocol

- Keep original audio and record its path here.
- Say the gallery and object identifier at the beginning of each note.
- Mark observed label text as quoted; do not paraphrase from memory later.
- If AI transcribes the notes, preserve raw audio and flag uncertain words.

### AI interview prompt

“Read only these field notes and my article brief. Interview me one question at a time. Ask for concrete sensory detail, then test where my interpretation exceeds the observation. Do not propose article prose or tell me what I believe.”

### Research conversion

For each museum fact that may enter the article, locate the museum record or another authoritative source and add it to the source catalog. Direct observation may support writer reflection; it does not verify historical attribution, date, technique, or institutional interpretation.

# Developmental editorial notes — “What Would André Bazin Make of AI Slop?”

## Editorial judgment

This version has found a concrete, finishable essay. The generated image—and especially its historically impossible
U-lock—gives the article an original scene through which to connect Bazin, AI, and authorship. The governing question
should now be:

> If AI performs much of the visible execution, where does the auteur reside?

The most promising answer already implicit in the draft is:

> The auteur is not located in the prompt or in manual execution alone. The auteur appears in the sustained exercise of
> judgment: conceiving the work, recognizing what is false or irrelevant, selecting and rejecting outputs, shaping their
> sequence, and accepting responsibility for the result.

This gives the essay a way to distinguish purposeful AI-assisted creation from “slop.” Slop is not merely imagery
produced by a model; it is imagery for which no one has exercised enough coherent judgment—or assumed enough
responsibility—to make the work answerable to an intention.

## What has improved

### The article now contains its own experiment

The U-lock is evidence of a specific encounter between your intention and the model's output. You asked for a
historically situated emotional moment; the model supplied a persuasive surface but failed to understand the world the
image was meant to inhabit.

More importantly, **you noticed the failure**. That act of noticing begins to answer “where is the auteur?” The model
generated; the human judged.

### The camera-obscura comparison now has a purpose

It can establish that authorship has never required making every mark without technical assistance. But AI is not simply
another camera obscura. An optical aid helps an artist perceive or trace a scene; a generative model can propose
content, form, detail, and style the user did not individually specify. That difference is why the location of
authorship becomes harder to identify.

### Bazin and *Bicycle Thieves* create the right pressure

De Sica did not become an auteur merely by placing a camera in Rome. His artistry lies in organizing bodies, locations,
performances, framing, duration, and sequence into a meaningful experience. This supplies the right standard for AI
direction: not “Who produced every frame?” but “Whose coherent judgment organizes the whole?”

## Move the auteur question forward

The title promises Bazin and “AI slop,” while most of the essay develops the image's relation to reality. The auteur
question appears only near the end. It currently arrives as a speculation instead of the conclusion of an argument.

Signal it earlier—perhaps after Rembrandt:

> New tools do not necessarily erase the artist. But when a tool begins generating the image rather than merely helping
> the artist make it, where should we locate the author?

This is suggested language, not replacement prose. Once the question is established, each section can advance it:

- Rembrandt: artistry has never been identical with unaided manual production.
- Bazin: mechanically produced images changed the relation among reality, tool, and artist.
- De Sica: direction consists in organizing many contributions into a coherent vision.
- Generated image: AI can execute a plausible surface without possessing the governing historical or dramatic judgment.
- U-lock: the human auteur becomes visible in recognizing, rejecting, and correcting what does not belong.
- Conclusion: AI relocates authorship toward judgment and responsibility; merely issuing a prompt does not demonstrate
  either.

## Crucial distinctions

### Auteur is not operator

A person who presses a shutter is not automatically a great photographer. A person who enters a prompt is not
automatically the auteur of everything returned. Operation initiates a process; authorship requires a more sustained
relation to the result.

### Auteur is not sole creator

Film is already collaborative. Directors work through actors, cinematographers, editors, designers, writers, locations,
and machines. Auteur language identifies a governing artistic intelligence, not someone who physically makes every
component. This is the most useful precedent for AI-assisted creation.

### Effort is not authorship

The estimate of 128,000 frames risks making the argument quantitative: enough regenerating might produce auteur status.
But a director does not individually construct each conventional film frame. The question is whether human judgment
governs the work across frames, scenes, and revisions.

### Coherence is not sufficient

A future system might maintain perfect visual and narrative continuity. That would make it a more capable production
system, but would not answer who chose the work's purpose, why these characters matter, what was rejected, or who stands
behind the finished claim.

### Authorship is not artistic merit

A work can have an identifiable auteur and still be bad. A generated work might move an audience even if its authorship
is distributed or unclear. Keep “Who authored this?” distinct from “Is this good art?” and “Does it possess Bazinian
realism?”

## What to retain and deepen

### Retain the U-lock

This is the sharpest detail in the article. Let the reader see why the image initially succeeds and why the error
matters. The anachronism exposes a distinction between statistical plausibility and situated understanding.

Then turn it back on the human creator: once you see it, what do you do? Regenerating is not yet artistry. You must know
why it is wrong, decide whether historical fidelity matters, determine what emotional or narrative purpose the object
serves, and keep making decisions consistent with the work as a whole.

### Deepen De Sica as director

Add one compact observation revealing direction as judgment. The ending of *Bicycle Thieves* may serve better than a
general summary: its meaning comes from how Ricci's failed theft, Bruno's witnessing, the crowd, and their final
movement together are arranged—not merely from the fact that a camera recorded them.

### Retain both continuity and break

- **Continuity:** artists have always used tools and collaborators; AI use does not automatically nullify authorship.
- **Break:** a generative model supplies far more unspecified content than an optical aid, and its output need not
  record the depicted event. Human contribution is therefore harder to see and more important to articulate.

The argument works because it neither dismisses AI as inherently inauthentic nor treats it as an ordinary neutral tool.

## What to compress or remove

- **Universal art history:** The cave-painting opening is broad, and its list mixes media, subjects, and objects. Begin
  closer to tools and authorship or reduce this history to two sentences.
- **Rembrandt:** Keep the painting, but use it mainly to establish that technical assistance does not exhaust artistic
  judgment. Do not imply unverified camera-obscura use.
- **Authenticity:** The draft leaps from AI lacking authenticity to real subjects being required for artistic merit.
  Painting, animation, fiction, and staged cinema resist that conclusion. Ask instead which human judgments give a
  nonexistent scene meaning and integrity.
- **Model promotion:** Remove “most Advanced ChatGPT 6.0 Astra.” It sounds promotional and will date quickly. Identify
  the system accurately and simply in a caption or note.
- **Frame count:** Remove it unless carefully sourced. It encourages the mistaken idea that authorship is proportional
  to frame-level labor. Discuss the qualitative challenge of maintaining historical, spatial, emotional, and narrative
  coherence.
- **Inline references:** Separate the Getty and film citations from the prose. The pasted version may have lost Medium
  formatting, so verify this in the published layout.

## A finishable structure at 900–1,100 words

The current file is about 934 words including captions and references. It does not need substantial expansion; it needs
redistribution and a conclusion.

1. **Tool or author? — 100–130 words.** Use Rembrandt or the camera obscura to establish the question quickly.
2. **Bazin's useful distinction — 140–180 words.** Photography's automatic process did not remove the artist; it changed
   what the artist controlled and gave the image a distinctive relation to what stood before the lens.
3. **What De Sica contributes — 170–220 words.** Use one scene to show the auteur organizing reality, not merely
   recording it.
4. **The AI experiment — 220–270 words.** Show the initial plausibility and the U-lock, then explain what sustained
   human direction would demand.
5. **Where the auteur goes — 170–220 words.** Locate AI-assisted authorship in conception, selection, revision,
   arrangement, disclosure, and responsibility.

## The ending the essay has earned

The draft currently stops after inviting comparison between the generated image and the film still. It needs to return
to “AI slop” and answer provisionally.

A strong conclusion should:

1. Admit that the U-lock can be fixed and future systems will make fewer obvious errors.
2. Explain that eliminating errors does not produce an auteur; polish and authorship differ.
3. Locate the human auteur in a pattern of decisions and responsibility across the finished work.

### Suggested closing passage

This is suggested language, not replacement prose:

> The U-lock is an easy mistake to repair, and future models may not make it. But removing errors is not the same as
> directing a film. De Sica's authorship does not reside in any single frame of *Bicycle Thieves*; it emerges from the
> judgments that bind performances, places, incidents, and images into a way of seeing human desperation. AI can now
> supply more and more of those images. The auteur, if there is one, must still decide what belongs, what does not, and
> what the work is finally asking us to see.
>
> Perhaps “AI slop” names work in which that judgment is absent—or at least nowhere evident. The question is no longer
> simply whether AI touched the image. It is whether anyone remained answerable for the whole.

The final sentence is a strong landing point because it turns authorship from a claim of ownership into a practice of
responsibility.

## Checks before publication

- Spell **André Bazin** with the accent.
- Describe Bazin's photographic ontology as a special causal relation to what stood before the camera, not neutral
  capture of reality “as it is.”
- Verify and cite “a hallucination that is also a fact” in the edition used.
- Verify the camera-obscura claims and avoid implying Rembrandt used one.
- Verify the properties attributed to painting on copper, especially that correction was “easy.”
- Use **Antonio Ricci** once before shortening it to Ricci.
- Treat non-professional actors, location shooting, black-and-white photography, and camera style as choices rather than
  automatic realism.
- Identify the image-generation system accurately without comparative marketing language.
- Verify the U-lock claim, distinguishing patent date from invention, manufacture, or widespread use.
- Remove or source the frame count.
- Use **auteur** carefully. Bazin's relationship to the *politique des auteurs* was critical and nuanced; do not imply
  that his photographic ontology straightforwardly supplies an auteur theory. Bazin can frame the image's relation to
  reality, while cinema's distributed production frames the authorship question.

## Revision sequence to reach a finish

### Pass 1 — Write the ending first

Draft your own 100–150 word answer to “where is the auteur?” before revising the opening. The ending will reveal which
earlier material the essay needs.

### Pass 2 — Build a one-sentence spine

Use this as a test, not prescribed language:

> From the camera obscura to cinema, tools have redistributed artistic labor; generative AI pushes that redistribution
> further, making the auteur visible not through unaided execution but through sustained, coherent, accountable judgment.

Every paragraph should establish, complicate, or demonstrate that sentence.

### Pass 3 — Make the U-lock the hinge

Move from Bazin and De Sica into the experiment, then let the anachronism turn the essay from image production to human
judgment. Do not treat it merely as proof that current models remain imperfect.

### Pass 4 — Cut before adding

Compress the art history, reduce Rembrandt speculation, remove the promotional model description, and reconsider the
frame count. Use the recovered space for one specific De Sica choice and the conclusion.

### Pass 5 — Audit the key vocabulary

Check every use of “real,” “reality,” “authenticity,” “artist,” “author,” and “auteur.” Each should name a specific
relationship, not serve as a synonym for “good,” “human,” or “believable.”

### Pass 6 — Copyedit and format

Only after the argument is stable, repair sentence mechanics, accents, film-title formatting, captions, and citations.

## Definition of done

The essay is ready when a reader can answer these without guessing:

1. What did Bazin think was distinctive about the photographic image?
2. What does De Sica contribute beyond pointing a camera at reality?
3. What did the AI generate, and what judgment did it fail to exercise?
4. Where does the essay ultimately locate the auteur in AI-assisted work?

If the answer to the fourth is “in sustained, coherent, accountable judgment,” the draft should show that through the
U-lock and *Bicycle Thieves*, not merely state it at the end.

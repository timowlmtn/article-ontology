# Article brief

- **Working title:** The first proof is the ugly one
- **Central question:** What becomes visible when we move from the syntax of first-order predicate calculus to satisfaction and truth in a structure—and what does that movement reveal about the difference between a valid formal proof, a checked artifact, and human understanding?
- **Why this matters:** AI systems can search for formal proof steps and interact with proof assistants. Their success makes it easy to confuse a well-formed expression, semantic truth in a model, syntactic derivability, a verified formal artifact, and an explanation that reveals why a statement holds.
- **Intended reader:** AI users and mathematically curious general readers.
- **Tentative thesis:** Not yet decided. A possible direction is that correctness is necessary but does not exhaust mathematical understanding; elegance may arise through the human work of finding a useful structure, explanation, and transfer.
- **Thesis confidence:** low; framing
- **Scope:** A concise essay anchored in one problem and one formal-verification lineage, with one current AI theorem-proving comparison.
- **Proposed focal theorem:** The compactness theorem for first-order logic: if every finite subset of a set of first-order sentences is satisfiable, the full set is satisfiable. Its proof is the later test case because it follows the book's progression from formal language to semantics and then to a theorem with real intermediate machinery.
- **Out of scope:** Explaining all of model theory; assessing whether AI is conscious, creative, or a mathematician; claiming that formal proof assistants establish mathematical truth independently of their formalization and trusted foundations; surveying all AI theorem provers.
- **Writer's existing beliefs:** A clumsy but correct proof can become more elegant as humans discover a shorter, clearer, or more general route; AI may initially produce or help locate valid but opaque arguments, while also helping humans investigate their structure.
- **What could change my mind:** Evidence that formal verification already gives an adequate account of mathematical understanding, or that compactness is too technically distant from the intended reader to carry the article's aesthetic question.
- **Key terms that may be ambiguous:** language; structure; satisfaction; truth; semantic consequence; formal validity; derivation; verification; explanation; understanding; elegance; beauty; AI-assisted proof.
- **Likely strongest counterargument:** Mathematical beauty is neither a stable property nor an evidentiary standard. A proof can be long, computer-aided, and mathematically profound; treating elegance as necessary risks excluding legitimate mathematics.
- **Desired reader movement:** From “a proof is a chain of correct symbols” to a sharper question: what do symbols mean in a structure, what does a formal system establish, who or what checks it, and what remains to be understood or appreciated?
- **Form / frame:** A concept-led essay that earns its later case through close reading of one mathematical text.
- **Current stage:** framing; initial reading sequence selected

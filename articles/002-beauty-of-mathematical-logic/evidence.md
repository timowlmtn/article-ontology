# Prospective evidence — Beauty of mathematical logic

These are research hypotheses and reading targets. No source below has been accepted by the writer or read with article-ready locators.

## CLM-001 — Syntax, satisfaction, truth, and derivability are related but distinct

- **Prospective claim:** A first-order formula's formation, a structure's satisfaction of it, a sentence's truth in a structure, a derivation's formal status, a proof assistant's successful check, and a person's understanding answer different questions and should not be collapsed.
- **Support needed:** SRC-041 for the syntax/semantics distinctions; SRC-043 for the scope of kernel checking.
- **Challenge:** In some formalist accounts, proving and mathematical knowledge may be described more tightly; do not treat a common-language distinction as a settled philosophy of mathematics.
- **Scope / qualification:** The article needs a practical distinction for readers, not a final resolution of philosophy of mathematics.
- **Status:** needs-reading

## CLM-002 — Formal verification gives strong conditional assurance, not automatic interpretation

- **Prospective claim:** A proof assistant can check that a proof term establishes a formal theorem from the definitions, axioms, and imported results available to it; this does not alone show that the formal statement perfectly captures the intended informal problem.
- **Support needed:** SRC-043, with exact wording and its stated trust boundary.
- **Challenge:** Do not use this qualification to dismiss formal verification's real epistemic value.
- **Status:** needs-reading

## CLM-003 — Compactness may make the syntax/semantics relation concrete

- **Prospective claim:** The compactness theorem may show why no single finite inspection of sentences exhausts the semantic question of whether an infinite set has a model.
- **Support needed:** SRC-041's statement and proof, plus a later explanatory source if needed.
- **Challenge:** The theorem's technical proof may be too abstract for the essay's readers; a smaller lemma or worked example may carry the conceptual weight better.
- **Scope / qualification:** Do not state or explain the theorem from memory; defer all prose until the book's treatment is read.
- **Status:** needs-reading

## CLM-004 — AI theorem provers search or propose within a formally checked environment

- **Prospective claim:** In systems built around Lean, learned components may choose premises or propose proof steps, while the proof assistant's kernel checks the resulting proof term.
- **Support needed:** SRC-043, SRC-044, and SRC-045.
- **Challenge:** System architectures vary; no claim about one system applies automatically to all AI-assisted mathematical work.
- **Status:** needs-reading

## CLM-005 — Elegance may be a practice of explanation and compression, not a reward for bare correctness

- **Writer idea to test:** A first correct proof can be unwieldy, and later work may make its governing idea, reusable structure, or surprising connection visible.
- **Evidence needed:** Mathematicians' accounts of proof elegance and at least one counterexample where length or complexity is itself illuminating.
- **Challenge:** “Elegant” is historically and personally variable; it may have no single criterion.
- **Status:** question, not a claim

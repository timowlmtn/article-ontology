# Article research questions

Translate the brief into answerable questions. A question earns a place here when its answer could change the thesis, the argument, or the fairness of the article.

## Required question types

Before research is mature, include at least one question in each relevant category:

- **Definition:** What do key terms mean in each discipline or source?
- **History:** Where did the central idea originate, and how did it change?
- **Mechanism:** How is the claimed effect supposed to occur?
- **Application:** What happens in a concrete work, case, object, or experiment?
- **Counterargument:** What is the strongest serious challenge?
- **Boundary:** When does the thesis not apply?
- **Verification:** Which facts, quotations, dates, or causal claims need primary evidence?

## Exit criteria

- The terms carrying the argument have explicit, source-grounded distinctions.
- The strongest counterargument has been researched rather than imagined vaguely.
- Major prospective claims have evidence requirements.
- Important gaps are visible and have a next action or an explicit scope decision.

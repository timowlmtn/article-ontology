# How can a 20th century film critic help us critique AI

Humans have created art earliest cave paintings to express the events around them being alive. Art
represents creations from our experience and imagination that capture our emotions, likenesses, fantasies,
and thoughts. The media has always developed alongside technology: cave paintings, sarcophagi,
daily life, plays, sacred texts, and the lives of the artists themselves.

For example, "Rembrandt Laughing" is a self-portrait that he captured of himself with a masterful sense
of capturing a single moment in time. The image feels real in a way that we can relate to: through a smile,
the moment right before one begins laughing, the playful addition of a gorget to give the subject panache.
He captures himself in a timeless moment. We don't know if he used a camera obscura to make such a realistic
painting, but it does have a realistic quality in the way that a photograph would.

At the time when he painted that self-portrait, many painters were using the camera obscura to enhance
their work, but they weren't necessarily public about. Today, many authors are using AI and we often
do not truly know the involvement of AI in their work.

https://www.getty.edu/podcasts/art-and-ideas/anne-woollett-on-rembrandt-laughing/

The camera obscura evolved into the camera and art that was product of paint and canvas diverged from
a media (was it an art?) that involved a camera, a light source, and a subject. Andre Bazin explored
the idea of how captured reality in specific way and turned its capture into art. In his essay, "The
Ontology of the Photographic Image," he puts forth that the artistry of the photograph or film by way
of the intention and the way that we react to an image when we view it.

In his analysis, Bazin references the film "The Bicycle Thief" and notes that _nothing happens that might
as well have not happened_. The film's artistry lies not so much in its realism as in its artistic
way that each scene carries the story forward in such a way that we feel Ricci's hopes, his desperation,
his shame, his fundamental human emotions, because we do feel them. And that placement of the camera,
the depiction of the scene is the artistic core of the film.

To trace back then, Rembrandt used camera obscura in his works, Vittorio De Sica used a different kind of artistry
in his films. In both cases the artist makes a feel something. Rembrandt with the joy of a laugh, and
De Sica with the desperation of poverty. The two very different mediums share the property that they both
communicate to us a fundamental human emotion.

Is AI simply a continuation of the technological advances along the same lines as a camera obscura or a film camera?
It extends the tools a creator has, in the sense that you can use it to make things. Is there
an inherent flaw within the AI, because when it creates something, it is not created by a person? What if we trained
the AI to create a masterpiece of film like The Bicycle Thief? I think it could create images and scenes that trace
through Ricci's trials and convey the true tragic human emotions that he feels.

Bazin talks about the power of the film to capture a world that is at once a reality at moment in time, and also
an image - a hallucination that is also a fact. We believe in the stories films tell us, because the director
positions the cameras, the subjects, and the surroundings in such a way that each snippet conveys a message, and
whole of the snippets conveys an entire story.

In film, the director aligns scenes around an idea. Through choices of framing, performance, lighting, and sequence,
those scenes together become art. Bazin calls photography “a hallucination that is also a fact”: it gives us the
presence of a real thing, because the image remains tied to an event that occurred before the camera. But what becomes
of direction in an AI-generated film composed from learned representations rather than a filmed reality? If AI can
create the hallucination of a world with the same appearance of reality, where does the artistry reside—in the prompt,
the selection of images, the shaping of the sequence, or somewhere else?

The question of artistry changes when AI is involved. A generated image relies on the creative work of
many human sources.  An AI will not act on its own, and a person still supplies the ideas, sets the scenes, describes 
the goals, and reviews the product.  Is that person lesser of an artist because of the tools they use?  It is
a challenging question, because the AI is *generating* the images and movement toward the result.  Could the AI,
without basis in reality, convey the emotions authentically that the viewer believes?

# Deep dive 003 — Auteur, authorship, and collaboration

## Purpose

Recover auteurism as a historical critical dispute, then build a reusable vocabulary for creative agency in collaborative and AI-mediated work.

## Bounded question

When critics call someone an auteur, are they attributing style, control, intention, value, responsibility, ownership, or some unstable combination?

## Why reuse this

The distinctions apply to film, software, design, generative systems, agents, studios, and any work whose production exceeds a single maker.

## Core references

- SRC-003 — [Astruc, “The Birth of a New Avant-Garde: La Caméra-Stylo”](https://www.newwavefilm.com/about/camera-stylo-astruc.shtml): verify the translation before quotation.
- SRC-011 — Bazin, “De la politique des auteurs,” in *Selected Writings*: use Bazin's own intervention to complicate auteur worship.
- François Truffaut, [“A Certain Tendency of French Cinema”](https://doi.org/10.1525/9780520957411-043): catalog after writer review; read as a polemic in its historical setting.
- SRC-004 — [Sarris, “Notes on the Auteur Theory in 1962”](https://alexwinter.com/media/pdfs/andrew_sarris_notes_on_the-auteur_theory_in_1962.pdf): identify his criteria rather than treating “auteur” as a synonym for director.
- SRC-014 — [Kael, “Circles and Squares”](https://online.ucpress.edu/fq/article/16/3/12/37901/Circles-and-Squares): read as a direct challenge to Sarris.
- SRC-017 — [Zylinska, *AI Art*](http://openhumanitiespress.org/books/download/Zylinska_2020_AI-Art.pdf): situate AI art within longer histories of mediation and automation.
- Boundary source: SRC-019 — [U.S. Copyright Office AI report](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/): compare legal human authorship with critical authorship only if useful.

## Reading sequence

Read chronologically as interventions:

1. Astruc: what becomes possible when cinema is treated as a language or pen?
2. Truffaut: what institution, tradition, and kind of screenplay is he attacking?
3. Bazin: what does he preserve and what excess does he resist?
4. Sarris: how does a policy become a theory and evaluative hierarchy?
5. Kael: which assumptions and critical consequences does she contest?
6. Only then apply the dispute to an AI-mediated work.

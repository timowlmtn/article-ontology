# Evidence — Benjamin, technological reproducibility, and AI authorship

These are prospective claims and reading targets. SRC-040 is a candidate source and has not yet been close-read in the preferred edition. No quotation below should enter prose without edition-specific verification.

## BCLM-001 — Reproducibility transforms function, not only quantity

- **Prospective finding:** Benjamin argues that technological reproducibility alters the authority, reception, production, and political function of art, not merely the number of copies available.
- **Support needed:** SRC-040 on authenticity, tradition, cult value, exhibition value, and film.
- **Challenge:** A summary centered only on “loss of aura” may erase the essay's dialectical and political structure.
- **Scope / qualification:** Historical interpretation; not yet an AI claim.
- **Status:** needs-reading

## BCLM-002 — Aura is relational

- **Prospective finding:** Aura should be understood through relations among presence, distance, uniqueness, tradition, and reception rather than as a free-floating quality or synonym for “human-made.”
- **Support needed:** Every definition and example in SRC-040, with locators.
- **Challenge:** Benjamin uses aura across several works and contexts; the “Work of Art” essay may not yield one exhaustive definition.
- **Scope / qualification:** Do not generalize beyond the selected version without comparison.
- **Status:** needs-reading

## BCLM-003 — Film production already fragments agency

- **Prospective finding:** Benjamin's film actor performs before an apparatus; testing, camera position, and editing mediate the performance before it reaches a mass audience. The finished work is therefore not reducible to direct execution by one person.
- **Support needed:** SRC-040 sections on film performance, apparatus, and editing.
- **Challenge:** Benjamin's analysis does not by itself endorse auteur theory or identify the director as sole organizing intelligence.
- **Scope / qualification:** Use to complicate manual-creation accounts, not to equate film production with AI generation.
- **Status:** needs-reading

## BCLM-004 — AI complicates the original/copy relation

- **AI inference:** A generated image can be indefinitely reproduced, but its initial production is not simply the duplication of one prior original. Benjamin's framework must therefore be combined with a technical account of training, inference, conditioning, and selection.
- **Support needed:** SRC-040 and one accepted primary model source.
- **Challenge:** “Recombination” may also oversimplify model behavior and the legal or aesthetic status of training material.
- **Scope / qualification:** System-specific claim required.
- **Status:** hypothesis

## BCLM-005 — Benjamin broadens the auteur question beyond individual intention

- **AI inference:** If AI-assisted authorship is located in sustained judgment, Benjamin adds a necessary challenge: production and reception are also shaped by apparatus, institutions, labor, markets, and politics.
- **Support needed:** SRC-040's apparatus and political sections; comparison with deep dive 003.
- **Challenge:** Benjamin's terminology and auteur criticism arise from different debates; the connection must be argued rather than attributed to him.
- **Scope / qualification:** Project interpretation, not a Benjamin quotation or settled scholarly conclusion.
- **Status:** hypothesis

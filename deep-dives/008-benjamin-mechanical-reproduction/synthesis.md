## Working distinctions

| Term | Provisional use | Must not be reduced to |
|---|---|---|
| Technological reproducibility | A historical transformation in how works are produced, copied, circulated, and received | cheap copying alone |
| Authenticity | The authority bound to a work's unique existence, history, and tradition | factual accuracy or moral sincerity |
| Aura | A relational structure involving presence, distance, uniqueness, tradition, and perception | beauty, quality, scarcity, or “human touch” |
| Cult value | A work's embeddedness in ritual or restricted presence | religious subject matter alone |
| Exhibition value | A work's orientation toward display and circulation | popularity alone |
| Apparatus | The technical and institutional arrangement mediating production and reception | one machine or model |
| Auteur | A contested attribution of organizing agency | sole causal contribution or legal ownership |

## Provisional relevance to article 001

Benjamin may help the article move from “Is AI another artistic tool?” to “How does this apparatus redistribute what can be seen, repeated, credited, and controlled?” He can strengthen the claim that the human auteur is not defined by manual execution. He also prevents the argument from locating everything in individual judgment by drawing attention to systems, labor, institutions, audiences, and politics.

The likely limit is equally important: generative AI does not merely reproduce a finished original, and Benjamin does not supply a ready-made theory of model training or AI authorship. The comparison must be built through a documented workflow rather than asserted through the word “reproduction.”

# Deep dive 001 — Ontology across meanings

## Purpose

Build a reusable distinction among philosophical ontology, Bazin's ontology of the photographic image, and computational ontology. The goal is not a single universal definition; it is the ability to notice when the same word is doing different work.

## Bounded question

What question does each use of “ontology” answer, what entities or commitments does it introduce, and what would count as a category error when moving among them?

## Why reuse this

This foundation should support every later article that moves between metaphysics, art, information organization, and AI architecture.

## Core references

- SRC-010 — [“Metaphysics,” Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/metaphysics/): orient to ontology as the study of being as such; do not treat one reference entry as the whole field.
- SRC-001 — [Bazin, “The Ontology of the Photographic Image”](https://online.ucpress.edu/fq/article/13/4/4/37551/The-Ontology-of-the-Photographic-Image): determine what makes this an ontological inquiry rather than a general theory of pictures.
- SRC-009 — [W3C, *OWL 2 Web Ontology Language Primer*](https://www.w3.org/TR/owl-primer/): identify the artifact, its domain, its statements, and its reasoning purpose.
- Later comparison: Gruber and Guarino from the project-wide Q-001 reading list.

## One-session reading plan

1. Read SRC-010 §§1–2 selectively. Write the question philosophical ontology asks.
2. Read SRC-001 fully. Mark every passage about production, resemblance, psychology, time, preservation, and belief.
3. Read SRC-009 §§1–3. Write what an OWL ontology is for and what it cannot represent by itself.

## Questions to answer

- Does Bazin use ontology to identify an essence, a genesis, a relation, a psychology, or several of these?
- Is a computational ontology a claim about what really exists or a designed artifact for a bounded domain?
- Does a generative model possess an ontology, learn representations that can be interpreted ontologically, or merely get described with ontological language?
- Which bridge among these meanings is historical, which is analogical, and which is an argument you must make?

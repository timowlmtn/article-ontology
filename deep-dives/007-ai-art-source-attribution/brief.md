# Brief — AI art, source attribution, and aesthetic judgment

## Research objective

Determine what one empirical study can establish about the relationship among an artwork’s true production source, a viewer’s source attribution, and aesthetic liking.

## Included

- Grassini and Koivisto’s 2024 study design, stimuli, measures, results, and stated limitations
- distinctions among actual source, perceived source, disclosure, provenance, and evaluation
- evidence that confirms, qualifies, or challenges the reported attribution pattern
- bounded relevance to criticism of AI-generated images

## Excluded unless a later article needs them

- a universal verdict on AI art’s quality or value
- claims that all viewers can or cannot identify AI-generated images
- legal conclusions about authorship, copyright, or disclosure obligations
- causal claims about moral objection, labor concerns, or model training data without direct evidence

## Completion condition

A source-grounded note can explain the study accurately, state its limits, and distinguish its attribution pattern from a randomized disclosure experiment or general claim about art.

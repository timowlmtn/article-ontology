# Evidence — AI art, source attribution, and aesthetic judgment

These are provisional records from a candidate source. They require writer acceptance and locator-level verification before supporting article prose.

## AAB-001 — The procedure blinds participants to the true image source

- **FACT CLAIM:** Participants viewed 40 artworks in random order—20 Midjourney v4 images and 20 human-made works—without being told each image’s true origin.
- **SOURCE CLAIM:** Participants answered four 0–100 questions for every image, including liking, positive emotion, inferred human/AI source, and familiarity.
- **SUPPORT:** SRC-032, Materials and Procedure.
- **SCOPE / QUALIFICATION:** The human works were selected by the researchers from copyright-free material and adapted to match the AI images’ square format; the study’s stimuli are not a representative sample of all human and AI art.
- **STATUS:** needs-locator-verification

## AAB-002 — Participants did not reliably discriminate source in this image set

- **FACT CLAIM:** The paper reports that participants could not discriminate the human-made and AI-generated sources reliably; the mean source judgments for both groups fell on the human-made side of the scale.
- **SUPPORT:** SRC-032, Results.
- **CHALLENGE:** This result is specific to the 40 images, their presentation, and the sample; it does not establish that people generally cannot identify AI-generated imagery.
- **STATUS:** needs-locator-verification

## AAB-003 — Actual source and believed source point in opposite evaluative directions

- **FACT CLAIM:** By actual source, the AI-generated images received higher mean liking ratings than the selected human-made images (59.2 versus 47.3 on the 0–100 scale).
- **SOURCE CLAIM:** The paper’s models report that images believed more likely to be AI-generated were liked less and elicited less positive emotion.
- **SUPPORT:** SRC-032, Results; Discussion.
- **CHALLENGE:** Actual source is a property of the stimulus sets, while believed source is a participant-level judgment. The latter was not randomly assigned as a label, so this study alone cannot identify the causal effect of disclosure.
- **SCOPE / QUALIFICATION:** Do not call this an intrinsic quality ranking or a universal anti-AI preference.
- **STATUS:** needs-locator-verification

## AAB-004 — “Negative bias” is the authors’ interpretation, not a complete explanation

- **SOURCE CLAIM:** The authors interpret the lower evaluation of images believed AI-generated as evidence of a negative bias toward AI-generated art.
- **SUPPORT:** SRC-032, Discussion and Conclusions.
- **ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS TO TEST:** Participants may infer lower effort, authenticity, intentionality, originality, ethics, or visual defects from their AI judgment. The paper’s design does not isolate those mechanisms.
- **STATUS:** needs-comparator-source

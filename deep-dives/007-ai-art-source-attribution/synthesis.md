# Synthesis — AI art, source attribution, and aesthetic judgment

**Status:** not ready. SRC-032 is a candidate source and needs writer acceptance plus locator-level checking.

## Working distinctions

| Term | Working meaning | Does not establish by itself |
|---|---|---|
| Actual source | How the image in fact was produced in the study | what a viewer believes about it |
| Perceived source | A viewer’s judgment that an image is human-made or AI-generated | verified provenance |
| Disclosure | Information supplied to a viewer about source | that the information is true, believed, or causally responsible for a rating |
| Aesthetic evaluation | A participant’s response, such as liking or positive emotion | objective quality, artistic value, or public consensus |
| Provenance | Documented evidence about an asset’s history and production | aesthetic reception or truth of depicted content |

## What the current evidence may permit

- In the study’s stimulus set, true source, perceived source, and liking did not move together in a simple way.
- The study is useful for posing a source-attribution question: what viewers *think* made an image can matter to their evaluation.

## What it does not yet permit

- A claim that labelling an identical image “AI-generated” causes lower liking.
- A general claim that people prefer AI art, prefer human art, or cannot identify AI-generated images.
- An explanation of the mechanism behind the attribution pattern.

## Reusable article implication

When an article invokes SRC-032, frame it as a bounded study of attribution and evaluation. Do not use it as proof that appearance reveals provenance, that provenance determines value, or that all AI-generated art is aesthetically disadvantaged.

# Deep dives

Use a deep dive for a bounded investigation that may support several articles: a contested definition, an architecture pattern, a historical thread, or a technical mechanism. Give it a brief, questions, relevant sources, and evidence appropriate to its scope. Do not let it become an unbounded dump of project context.

Copy `000-template/` for a new investigation. A deep dive may produce provisional conclusions, but each article must still select the evidence relevant to its own claims.

## A five-day working sequence

This is deliberately paced for thinking, not maximum reading volume. Stop when a source changes a distinction or raises a serious objection; record that before continuing.

### Day 1 — Name the ontologies

- Work in `001-ontology-meanings/` for 90–120 minutes.
- Read one philosophical definition, Bazin's essay, and the W3C primer selectively.
- Fill the comparison matrix without forcing a unified meaning.
- End by writing the most important category error the article must avoid.

### Day 2 — Read Bazin as critic

- Work in `002-bazin-representation-realism/` for two focused sessions.
- Read “The Ontology of the Photographic Image” and one essay on film language or montage.
- Watch or revisit one film sequence Bazin discusses.
- Record what the criticism shows that the abstract essay alone does not.

### Day 3 — Follow the auteur dispute

- Work in `003-auteur-authorship-collaboration/`.
- Read Astruc, Bazin, Sarris, and Kael as successive interventions, not textbook definitions.
- Make the authorship map before applying it to AI.
- End by separating credit, control, intention, responsibility, and ownership.

### Day 4 — Walk the museum

- Check the official RISD Museum “What's On View” page and current hours that morning.
- Use `005-criticism-museum-encounter/README.md` before, during, and immediately after the visit.
- Encounter first; read labels second; research object histories later.
- Preserve raw voice notes and write a short reflection before asking AI to synthesize.

### Day 5 — Understand the synthetic image

- Work in `004-synthetic-image-provenance/`.
- Read a primary diffusion-model paper only far enough to map training, conditioning, generation, and selection.
- Compare that production chain with a photographed encounter.
- Read C2PA's stated limits: provenance assertions are not truth judgments.
- Finish by revising the article thesis in your own words.

## End-of-day ritual

For each day, spend fifteen minutes updating only four things:

1. one claim that became stronger or narrower;
2. one distinction that must be preserved;
3. one unresolved challenge;
4. the smallest next action.

Then update the current article's `evidence.md`. Do not draft polished paragraphs merely to create a sense of completion.

## Using AI during the deep dives

- Before reading: ask for a source map and competing interpretations.
- During reading: ask for questions, never invented quotations or missing-page summaries.
- After reading: provide your notes and ask for an argument map, counterargument, or comparison table.
- After the museum: ask AI to interview you one question at a time before it offers synthesis.
- Before drafting: ask AI to audit the evidence burden and identify where analogy has replaced evidence.

The writer accepts sources, decides the thesis, and owns the final critical judgment.

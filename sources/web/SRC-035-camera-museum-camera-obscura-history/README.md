## What this source supports

The camera obscura has no single inventor because its basic operation is a natural optical phenomenon: a small opening
in a darkened space projects an inverted image of the scene outside. The museum credits Aristotle with observing the
phenomenon in the fourth century BCE, identifies Leonardo da Vinci's fifteenth-century explanation, and describes
sixteenth- and seventeenth-century additions of lenses and mirrors that made it a more practical drawing instrument.

## Citation guidance and limits

- Use to support the concise claim that the camera obscura was developed over centuries rather than invented by one
  person.
- Do not use this page alone to establish the earliest worldwide written description. Sources often also identify the
  Chinese philosopher Mozi as an early observer; that specific comparative claim needs a source that treats the Chinese
  and Greek records directly.
- For detailed histories of Ibn al-Haytham, lenses, or the phrase *camera obscura*, use specialist history-of-science
  sources.

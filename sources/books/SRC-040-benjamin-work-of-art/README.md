## Version warning

Benjamin repeatedly revised this essay. The commonly cited English title “The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction,” translated by Harry Zohn and collected in Hannah Arendt's *Illuminations*, generally points to a later version and uses different wording and pagination. The French version published in 1936 was the only version printed during Benjamin's lifetime.

Do not combine quotations, section numbering, titles, or page locators from different versions. For this project, begin with the expanded second version in *Selected Writings*, volume 3. Record the *Illuminations* text separately if the writer later chooses to compare versions or use its more familiar title.

## Why this edition

The second version contains important material on first and second technology, play, the apparatus, and film that is especially useful for a comparison with generative systems. Its scholarly framing also makes the version explicit. The preferred title “technological reproducibility” keeps open Benjamin's concern with a historical condition of repeatability rather than narrowing it prematurely to machines that duplicate finished originals.

## Relevance to the Bazin/AI article

Benjamin can deepen the article's auteur question in three ways:

- He treats technological change as transforming the social function of art, not merely giving an artist a new neutral tool.
- His film actor performs for an apparatus and is assembled through testing and editing, complicating any simple location of authorship in bodily execution.
- His analysis asks how reproduction changes authority, reception, ownership, and politics—dimensions that the word “auteur” can otherwise collapse.

But Benjamin does not directly answer who the auteur of an AI-assisted work is. Applying his concepts to generative AI is an interpretation requiring an account of the model, training corpus, interface, human selection, and platform context.
